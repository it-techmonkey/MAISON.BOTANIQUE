# Requirements, feedback and proposals

## Confirmed needs

- One Maison workspace, initially for the two business owners; other staff access may follow.
- See completed work, unfinished work, blockers, decisions, responsibility, next steps and the business outcome.
- Preserve evidence, sources and settled decisions so the same questions are not repeatedly reopened.
- Support useful department coverage and an easy personal working view.
- Make the interface approachable, interactive and informative without requiring a long explanation.
- Preserve work incrementally in a review branch/worktree.

## Owner feedback, in order

1. Recommending capabilities already in the existing proposal did not advance the design.
2. Changing colors while keeping the same information architecture was insufficient.
3. Claude’s design was preferred visually; evolve its identity rather than replace it.
4. The combined workbench was too crowded. Text volume obscured purpose, navigation and relationships.
5. Decisions and evidence are valuable; their placement and reading burden need improvement.

The original qualitative feedback included: “It feels overly clustered into a single, confusing view.”

## Preserve

- Warm paper/ink visual language and restrained wine accents from the preferred reference.
- Business and department context, accountable human ownership and optional AI assistance.
- Dependencies, next actions, source evidence, settled decisions and review history.
- A clear distinction between reported work and verified results.
- Local-only prototype interactions until an implementation scope is approved.

## Rejected or deferred

- Repeating the same layout with cosmetic changes.
- A permanent overview, task board and long knowledge panel competing at once.
- A dense first screen with many filters, pins, focus controls and technical status labels.
- Treating checklist ticks, implementation reports or merges as proof of the entire business outcome.
- Installing tools merely to compensate for unclear navigation.

## Latest proposal — not yet approved

- Three main destinations: Business, My day and Reference.
- A department overview with one next move per department.
- A clear missing requirement → responsible person → next-action route.
- Dedicated task pages with supporting information revealed on request.
- Retain underlying evidence and decisions; simplify their default presentation.
- Delay integrations and additional productivity features until the basic workflow is understandable.

## Governance

Public fixtures are fictional and are never operational authority. Exact originals stay local and ignored.
New decisions supersede older ones explicitly; history is retained. App implementation, live access and production
release each require their own agreed scope. Do not infer them from approval to save the design work.
